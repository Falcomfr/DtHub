Several DOFUS Touch accounts side by side on Windows

From your own phones. Not an emulator: the game runs on your real phone, DT Hub mirrors it onto the PC and arranges the windows.

Your accounts on screen

You install DOFUS Touch on the phone's main profile, then again on a cloned profile such as Second Space. Each profile holds its own account, and DT Hub finds every one of them.

Side by side

Each account gets its own window, at the same size and place, so they overlay exactly. Ctrl+Tab cycles through them.

Or inside one window

Any account docks into a tabbed frame, is reordered by dragging, or pulled back out. Typing and the clipboard reach it exactly as they reach a free window.

One panel to drive it all

Shown or hidden with Ctrl+P. Your phones, the accounts each one hosts, and a click to open or close any of them.

It shows the Android profile behind each account, the phone's battery level, and says what is wrong when something is: low battery, phone heating up, 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi, pairing to redo.

From the keyboard

ShortcutAction
Ctrl+PShow or hide the panel
Ctrl+TabNext account, or next tab
Ctrl+TTile the windows side by side
Ctrl+1 … Ctrl+5Four sizes, then full screen
Ctrl+RPut every window back in place
Ctrl+QShow or hide the guides

They only act while a DT Hub window is focused, so Ctrl+Tab keeps working in your browser. All of them can be changed.

Guides beside the game

Ctrl+Q opens the guides from papycha.fr, displayed with their agreement. They follow a quest step by step and remember where you stopped.

And enough to find your way

782 quests, 83 dungeons, 2 raids and 8 lairs, sorted by level, size and area, with their coordinates and their prerequisites.

What you need

  • Windows 10 or 11, 64 bit.
  • An Android 11 phone or later, with a cloned profile already created. DT Hub does not create one.
  • An internet connection on first launch, once. 19 MB of third party tools from Google and Genymobile, each checked against a known fingerprint before use.

Windows will warn you the first time

SmartScreen shows "Windows protected your PC" on any downloaded program it does not recognise. Click More info, then Run anyway.

The file is not signed, so its checksum is the only way to verify what you downloaded. One line in PowerShell, from the folder where both files sit: 

(Get-FileHash DtHub.exe -Algorithm SHA256).Hash -eq (Get-Content DtHub.exe.sha256).Split(' ')[0]

True means it is the right file. Why it is unsigned is written in docs/CONFIANCE.md, in French.
